Two formulas from my bot breakdowns can become one strategy

Take ROC + ATR, a combination similar to one I've covered

ROC (Rate of Change) measures the percentage movement over a chosen window:

ROC = (Current Price - Price N Candles Ago) / Price N Candles Ago Ã 100

BTC rises from $60,000 to $60,600? ROC is +1%, but that alone isn't a trading signal.

ATR (Average True Range) measures the normal size of price swings:

TR = max(High - Low, |High - Previous Close|, |Low - Previous Close|)

ATR = Average TR Over N Candles

If ATR is $300 and BTC moves $600, that movement equals 2 ATR. The idea is to filter ordinary noise instead of reacting to every candle.

Aroon measures something different: how recently the strongest high or low appeared.

Aroon Up = 100 Ã (N - Periods Since Highest High) / N

Aroon Down = 100 Ã (N - Periods Since Lowest Low) / N

Aroon Oscillator = Aroon Up - Aroon Down

Aroon Up above Aroon Down suggests a bullish bias. But where are the entry, exit and risk limits? That's what turns an indicator into a strategy

Now make AI try to break the strategy it just built

The first backtest is a draft. Before deployment, I'd run four tests:

1. Move the thresholds. If ROC(10) works, try ROC(8) and ROC(12). If 0.8 ATR works, try 0.6 and 1.0. An edge that disappears with a tiny change might be fitted to the past.

2. Remove one rule. Disable ATR, rerun the backtest, then disable the trend filter. This is ablation testing. It shows which conditions carry information and which simply make the code more complicated.

3. Change the regime. Compare trending, sideways and high-volatility periods. A momentum setup may work during sustained moves and struggle when price keeps reversing. Quant researchers also use Hidden Markov Models to study regimes; that doesn't mean Horizon has a built-in HMM module.

4. Stress execution. Increase fees and slippage. Then test the strategy on a historical period that wasn't used to tune it. If its edge disappears, that's more useful than a beautiful equity curve.
